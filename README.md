# AIOS

AIOS is an event-driven operating system for autonomous organizations. It coordinates organizations, human owners, AI employees, goals, tasks, events, institutional memory, authority, approvals, tools, resources, and audit records.

The first intended organization is a human-owned business researched, launched, and operated largely by AI. AIOS enables broad machine initiative without transferring legal or fiduciary accountability: humans retain ownership, reserve high-risk powers, and approve consequential or irreversible decisions.

## Constitutional design

AIOS begins with governance rather than automation. Its design principles are:

- Constitution before automation.
- Events before conversations.
- Institutional records before model memory.
- Least authority before convenience.
- Reversible actions before irreversible actions.
- Evidence before confidence.
- Human accountability remains final.
- Models are interchangeable resources, not the architecture.
- Every consequential action must be attributable and auditable.
- AI employees may create temporary specialist workers only within delegated authority and resource limits.

The constitutional documents are normative and versioned:

- [`constitution/CONSTITUTION.md`](constitution/CONSTITUTION.md) establishes sovereignty, governance, rights, duties, and amendment rules.
- [`constitution/ONTOLOGY.md`](constitution/ONTOLOGY.md) defines the shared entities and relationships.
- [`constitution/AUTHORITY.md`](constitution/AUTHORITY.md) defines authority, delegation, approvals, and action risk.
- [`constitution/MEMORY.md`](constitution/MEMORY.md) defines institutional record provenance, lifecycle, privacy, and retrieval.

The [`docs/VISION.md`](docs/VISION.md) and [`docs/ROADMAP.md`](docs/ROADMAP.md) documents describe the intended system and a staged path to controlled execution.

## Status

Version **0.0.1** is the constitutional foundation. It is a governance specification, not yet a working kernel. Where documents conflict, the Constitution controls, followed by the Authority and Memory policies, then operational policies and implementation.
