# AIOS Vision

**Version:** 0.0.1

## What AIOS is

AIOS is an event-driven operating system for autonomous organizations. It provides a governed substrate in which human owners and AI employees can turn missions into goals, goals into tasks, and tasks into attributable actions. It coordinates events, authority, approvals, tools, budgets, institutional memory, and audit records across long-running organizational work.

The first target is a human-owned business that AI largely researches, launches, and operates. The system should let AI employees show initiative, specialize, learn from institutional outcomes, and coordinate continuously, while humans retain final legal and fiduciary accountability and approve high-risk or irreversible decisions.

The architectural center is not a chatbot or a particular model. It is a durable event and governance kernel. Models are interchangeable reasoning resources assigned to roles. Conversations are interfaces that may propose work; accepted proposals become typed events and records. Organizational identity survives model replacement, context loss, restarts, and vendor change.

## Operating shape

AIOS is intended to make the following loop explicit and inspectable:

1. An authorized mission produces goals with evidence and success criteria.
2. AI employees propose plans and tasks within delegated authority and budgets.
3. Events route work to the appropriate role, tool, or human approval queue.
4. Policy evaluates authority, risk, reversibility, privacy, and resource constraints.
5. Approved actions execute through controlled tools and emit observations and audit records.
6. Outcomes update institutional memory, goal state, confidence, and future decisions.

This approach supports autonomy by making boundaries executable. Low-risk, reversible work can proceed quickly. Consequential work becomes a well-formed proposal for accountable approval. Temporary specialists can be created for narrow tasks, but inherit neither unlimited authority nor unlimited resources.

## What AIOS is not

AIOS is not:

- a claim that an AI system owns, governs, or bears legal responsibility for a business;
- a single autonomous agent with unrestricted credentials;
- a chat transcript used as a database or source of truth;
- a model orchestration framework whose identity depends on one provider;
- an excuse to automate decisions that law, ethics, or governance reserve to humans;
- a system that treats confidence, fluency, or consensus as evidence;
- a promise of unattended operation before controls and auditability exist; or
- a substitute for legal, accounting, security, safety, or domain expertise.

## Desired qualities

AIOS should be constitutionally governed, event-native, inspectable, model-agnostic, least-privileged, recoverable, privacy-aware, and economical. It should degrade safely: uncertainty leads to evidence gathering, authority gaps lead to escalation, budget exhaustion leads to pause, and control failure leads to suspension.

Success means a human owner can understand what the organization believes, why it is acting, who or what authorized it, what it spent, what changed, and how to stop or reverse it. Autonomy is valuable only when it remains attributable, bounded, and answerable to human sovereignty.
